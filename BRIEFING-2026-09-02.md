# Briefing — 2 September 2026

For review by someone without the session history. Written to be self-contained.

**Context.** Fyxx is a spirits and cigars retailer in Amman, Jordan. This is a
single-user marketing dashboard (React + Vite + TanStack Router + TypeScript on
Supabase) pulling from Klaviyo, LoyaltyLion and Shopify. One person uses it —
Naim, marketing. His manager Zeid sees only an exported monthly PDF, so the
report must be self-explaining.

The last stretch of work was a correctness pass: a filter audit, a verification
workbook, an order-level Shopify sweep, a retroactive-change repair, and a
critical review of every figure. What follows is the outcome of the final
items, and the decisions now open.

---

## 1. Apple Mail Privacy Protection — the largest unlabelled distortion

**The problem.** Apple Mail pre-fetches images and marks a message opened
whether or not anyone read it. So opens and open rates are inflated by an
unknown amount, and are **not comparable across time**, because the inflation
grows as Apple's share of the list grows. Nothing in the app, the monthly
report or the verification workbook said so. Opens appeared on four surfaces
including the report that goes to Zeid.

**Now labelled in all four places** — Campaigns, Flows and Push pages (one
shared component so the wording cannot drift), the monthly report, and the
workbook, where it leads the "Read me first" sheet above the four figures that
cannot be reconciled against their source.

**What replaces open rate was measured on our own 64 campaigns**, not taken as
received wisdom:

| | open rate | click rate |
|---|---|---|
| range | 26.9% – 89.0% | 0% – 10.2% |
| mean | 45.1% | 1.4% |
| relative spread (sd/mean) | 0.30 | **1.14** |
| correlation with revenue per delivered | 0.403 | **0.779** |
| correlation with order rate | 0.416 | **0.798** |

Clicks explain roughly **61%** of the variance in revenue; opens about **16%**.
Click rate carries 3.9× the relative spread, so it can rank campaigns where
open rate cannot. Opens barely predict clicks at all (0.359).

**The recommendation, and it is deliberately short:** judge a campaign on
revenue per delivered message, click rate, and orders attributed. That is the
entire list of metrics that survive scrutiny.

**Push is the exception.** Push opens are unaffected by Apple Mail, but Klaviyo
emits no push click event of any kind, so for push there is nothing to fall
back to — opens and conversions are the whole picture.

Reproduce with `scripts/diagnose/engagement-signal.mjs`.

## 2. Health was measuring the wrong thing

The health page checked **whether the job ran**, not **whether the data is
current**. Those are different questions and only the second is what anyone
means.

Proven on the live situation: on 2 September the `klaviyo_reach` job had
succeeded that morning, and its data reached only 26 August — seven days
behind. The page called it healthy. The monthly report caught the same problem
independently and refused to total reach, naming "3 of 31 days".

**Fixed.** Each source now carries a data-age threshold beside its job-age
threshold, and there is a new `behind` state distinct from `stale`, because the
fixes differ: a job that has not run needs restarting; a job that runs but lags
needs its backlog cleared.

    Shopify sales      state=ok      behind=0d
    Unique reach       state=behind  behind=7d  the job is succeeding, but its
                                                data only reaches 2026-08-26

## 3. Two empty tables — one merely unused, one actually broken

**Activations: unused, not broken.** Permissions are correct, the insert
payload matches the schema, and with zero rows the page degrades correctly to
"No activations for this month". It works; nobody has entered one.

**Reports: broken.** The report page saves its narrative with an upsert on
`(start_date, end_date)`, and no unique constraint on that pair existed.
Postgres rejects that with `SQLSTATE 42P10`. **The Save button had never been
able to work**, and nobody had noticed because the table is empty — so the
failure had never been triggered.

Proven with a rolled-back database probe rather than inferred, then fixed by
adding the constraint and re-probed to confirm it now succeeds.

That fix would have created a second problem: the Export page used a plain
insert, which would collide on the second export of a period. Switched to the
same upsert. That path was broken either way — before the constraint it would
have written duplicate rows and the report's `.maybeSingle()` read would have
thrown later.

## 4. Error handling — partly proven, with the limit stated plainly

Background: a failed query used to render as **"No campaigns in range."** — a
confident factual claim manufactured by a network error. That single behaviour
did more to damage trust in the dashboard than any wrong number.

**All twelve pages now have a reachable error branch.** Checked by ordering,
not merely presence: an error check placed after a loading check would be dead
code. One page flagged in that scan and proved to be a false positive.

**The mechanism is proven at the data layer.** Every failure mode produces an
error rather than empty data:

| broken query | result |
|---|---|
| missing table | throws |
| missing column | throws |
| empty date (the original trigger) | throws |
| empty date on the paged path | throws |

**Not proven: the rendered result in a browser.** The pages sit behind an
authentication guard and I did not enter credentials. So this is a structural
and data-layer proof, not the visual one that was asked for. To finish it,
someone logged in should clear a custom date on each page and confirm the error
block appears.

## 5. LoyaltyLion — a redundant import, and a decision

Their support confirmed four things, all matching what had already been
established independently: no programme-level analytics endpoint, exports are
UI-only, `merchant_id` is the Shopify customer id, and unrecognised query
parameters are silently ignored rather than erroring.

**They also said claimed rewards appear in the transactions export. True, and
stronger than stated.** The `/v2/transactions` endpoint — which is already
polled nightly — carries 2,363 claimed-reward records over exactly the span of
the manual CSV import. **Everything in the CSV is in the API, with 87 records
besides.** The CSV import is fully redundant and that table can become a live
source.

### The open decision: a webhook listener

LoyaltyLion has a `customer_claimed_reward` webhook. Shopify has an
`ORDERS_CANCELLED` one. Both would need something listening — an Edge Function
— which had been deferred on the grounds that it is unmonitored infrastructure.

**"How would I know if it stopped receiving?" has a clean answer.** Not "no
events in N days", which cannot distinguish a broken listener from a quiet
week. The answer is **reconciliation against the nightly poll that already
runs**: if the poll finds claimed rewards or cancellations the webhook never
delivered, that is known within 24 hours, with a count. A positive test rather
than an absence test.

**But that same fact is the argument against building it.** Both needs are
already met by polling — the nightly repair closes cancellation drift to zero,
and `/v2/transactions` already carries claimed rewards. The webhook's only gain
is latency, from 24 hours to seconds, and nothing on this dashboard consumes
that. Two separate needs pointing at one solution does not help when polling
already satisfies both.

**Recommendation: do not build it.** If that is overruled, the reconciliation
monitor above is what would make it safe.

## What a reviewer might usefully challenge

- Is "don't build the webhook" right, or is 24-hour latency worse than it
  looks for a shop where cancellations happen in batches on Mondays?
- Is the three-metric list (revenue per delivered, click rate, orders) too
  austere? Is there a defensible fourth?
- The data-age thresholds are judgement calls: 2 days for nightly sources,
  4 for reach, 9 for order influence, 40 for margin.
- The unproven half of the error-state work is real. Is a structural proof
  enough to move on, or should the browser check happen first?

## Standing constraints on this project

- Secrets come from a gitignored `.env` locally and GitHub Secrets in CI.
  Never commit a key, print one, or put one in frontend code.
- No destructive SQL without showing it first.
- The Shopify sync must stay strictly read-only. The token carries write
  scopes for future use; a guard asserts no mutation before every call.
- Do not connect Odoo — the seat limit is fully consumed.
- Do not reuse the store's existing legacy apps; they power live integrations.
